I use Earth observation data and machine learning to answer environmental questions at scale: where forests are being cleared, how much carbon farmland holds, and how glacial lakes are changing.

Most of my work sits at the point where imagery becomes a decision. The clearest picture of how I work is a couple of jobs described end to end.

Oct 2024 to present

Deforestation detection for EU compliance

Working student, geospatial analyst at Osapiens

The problem
Under the EU Deforestation Regulation, companies have to show their supply chains are not linked to forest loss. That proof depends on imagery being good enough to stand behind.
What I did
Ran quality checks across Planet and public satellite archives to work out which scenes were fit for deforestation detection. Annotated training data across several countries and supported the team on deforestation and plantation detection. Handled the spatial processing and cleaning in QGIS.
Why it matters
Detection is only as good as the imagery underneath it. Screening the data first keeps false alarms out of a process that has legal consequences for suppliers.

Feb to Aug 2024

Carbon accounting for European agroforestry

Intern, carbon flows in trees at Climate Farmers

The problem
Farmers planting trees on working land need a credible number for how much carbon those trees hold. Without one, the whole claim is an estimate nobody can check.
What I did
Built biomass estimation models and ran the sequestration calculations in R and Google Earth Engine across farms in several European countries, working from IPCC methodology rather than rules of thumb.
Why it matters
The output went straight to clients and the internal team as written reports, so the method had to be defensible to people who were not going to read the code.
